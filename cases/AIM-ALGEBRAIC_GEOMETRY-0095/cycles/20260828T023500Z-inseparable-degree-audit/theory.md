# Total-degree and separability audit

## Frozen target

For the imported irrationality-bound claim, isolate the covering-diagram step:
does its evaluation map have to inherit the inseparability of a rational
map to projective space? Audit this using the field definition and exact
monomial maps. The finite work concerns chosen maps on a rational surface,
not its minimum degree of irrationality, which is already 1.

Let k=F_p, K=k(u,v), with u,v algebraically independent. For positive
integers a,c and integer b>=0, set x=u^a*v^b, y=v^c and L=k(x,y).
The map of two-dimensional tori is (u,v) -> (x,y).
Its exponent row lattice Lambda is generated by (a,b),(0,c) in Z^2.
Total degree means [K:L]; separable degree counts generic geometric
points, and inseparable degree is their quotient, not a Jacobian nullity.
The census is p in {2,3,5}, a,c in {1,...,6}, b in {0,...,5}.

## Two approaches and the ordinary algebra argument

Approach A uses integral invariant factors: d1=gcd(a,b,c), d2=ac/d1.
The quotient lattice is Z/d1 plus Z/d2. Thus its index is ac.
Approach B constructs the subgroup and every coset in (Z/ac)^2 and counts
element orders directly. It does not use the invariant-factor formulas.
The verifier also counts kernels of the exponent map modulo p^r-1
for r=1,2 directly, rather than using gcd formulas.

Here is the field-degree bridge, separate from the finite experiments.
Laurent monomials in distinct Lambda cosets are linearly independent over
the fraction field of k[Lambda]: clear a common denominator and use their
disjoint exponent supports. A complete coset set spans after localizing
k[Z^2] over k[Lambda]. The resulting finite-dimensional domain over L is
a field, hence is K. Consequently [K:L]=[Z^2:Lambda]=ac.
Integral changes of monomial coordinates with determinant +/-1 are field
automorphisms. Smith normal form therefore reduces the map to the two
power maps of exponents d1,d2. A power exponent p^h*m with p not dividing m
has inseparable degree p^h and separable degree m. This gives
inseparable_degree=p^v_p(ac) and separable_degree=ac/p^v_p(ac).

On the torus, invertible diagonal coordinate scalings identify the Jacobian
rank with the rank of [[a,b],[0,c]] modulo p. This detects separability
when full rank, but not the whole p-power degree when rank deficient.
Over F_(p^r), the multiplicative group is cyclic of order p^r-1, so the
number of rational kernel points is gcd(d1,p^r-1)*gcd(d2,p^r-1).
This last count omits all infinitesimal multiplicity and can also omit
separable roots not rational over that field.

## Exact counterexamples

In characteristic 2, compare (u,v)->(u^2,v) and (u,v)->(u^4,v).
Both have Jacobian rank 1; their total/inseparable degrees are respectively
2 and 4. Each has a single rational kernel point over F_2 and F_4.
The second generic geometric fiber has one point with multiplicity 4:
in an algebraic closure, U^4-alpha=(U-alpha^(1/4))^4. The shortcut
p^(dimension-Jacobian rank) gives 2 rather than 4.

For a separable control, (u,v)->(u^3,v) in characteristic 2 has degree 3,
but its rational kernel count is 1 over F_2 and 3 over F_4. Thus even
separability does not justify substituting a finite-field fiber count
for total degree. All statements concern torus fibers, avoiding boundary
ramification or rational-map indeterminacy.

## Covering-diagram interpretation

The source map is recorded in sources/theory-source.md. Using that field
definition, take K_C=K, K_B=k(x), and the parameter t=y.
Then [K_C:K_B(t)]=ac, while the evaluation field inclusion K -> K_C is
identity and therefore separable. The projection field inclusion
K_B(t) -> K_C can be inseparable. Confusing these two arrows would wrongly
discard the examples above.

More generally, for any algebraic function field K/k of positive dimension
n and a transcendence basis x1,...,xn with finite extension degree e,
take K_C=K, K_B=k(x1,...,x_(n-1)), t=xn. The same identity evaluation gives
a field-diagram candidate of degree e. Via the published equivalence with
the proper-variety definition this gives scvg_k(X)<=irr_k(X), using total
degree. This is an ordinary proof from the definition, not a machine proof
of the complete-intersection bound. Smooth geometric fibers of the initially
chosen pencil have not been assumed or constructed.

  and equivalence distinguish the two arrows. Falsifier: source wording or
  diagram attaching separability to the other arrow.
