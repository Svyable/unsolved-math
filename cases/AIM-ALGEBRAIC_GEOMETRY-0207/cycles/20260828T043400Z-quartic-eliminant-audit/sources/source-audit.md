1. Tom Fisher, *On binary quartics and the Cassels-Tate pairing*, version 1,
   dated 31 August 2022, Section 2, printed page 2.
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/2208.14977v1
   Retrieved PDF text successfully. It defines unscaled quartic coefficients and
   the I,J formulas used here, gives determinant/scalar transformation weights,
   and uses Delta=16(4I^3-J^2)/27. The packet labels its smaller normalization D0.
   Fisher's equivalence permits a square scalar; divisor equivalence allows any
   nonzero scalar. Over C these coincide at that scalar step. This paper is not
   cited for surface realization or the packet's cross-ratio factorization.
   Published version: https://doi.org/10.1007/s40993-022-00376-z

2. M. Fryers, J. Y. Kaminski and M. Teicher, *Some applications of
   algebraic curves to computational vision*, v1, 15 October 2001, Section 3
   and Section 3.2, Theorem 1 and Lemma 1, printed pages 7-9.
   https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0110157v1
   Retrieved PDF text successfully. The setup uses complex geometry and images
   of a common smooth irreducible spatial curve; the theorem matches tangent
   epipolar-line divisors with multiplicities for generic cameras. This is useful
