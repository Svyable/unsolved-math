## Definitions and assumptions

Let phi=(1+sqrt(5))/2, so phi^2=phi+1 and 1<phi<2. Work in the simple-root
basis with Gram matrix [[1,-phi/2],[-phi/2,1]], which is positive definite.
Indices 0,1 are distinguished simple roots. The five positive roots are

    0=(1,0), 1=(0,1), 2=(phi,1), 3=(1,phi), 4=(phi,phi).

Reflection s1 sends (a,b) to (-a+phi*b,b), and s2 sends it to (a,-b+phi*a).
Their closure on the simple roots has ten vectors, the displayed five and
their negatives; s1*s2 has order five. Every root has Gram norm one. Thus this
is the specified equal-length I2(5) realization, not rounded trigonometric data.

The cone strict order is i<j when both coordinates of root j minus root i are
nonnegative and i!=j. An antichain is a subset with no comparable distinct pair.
H(s,t) sums s^(number of indices 0,1 in A)*t^(size of A) over all antichains A.
Do not interchange the two variables. Panyushev's permutation sends A to the
minimal elements outside the down-set generated by A. Orbits include the empty
antichain, and all labels are fixed throughout the finite computation.

For abstract size m>=3, the target is 1+2st+(m-2)t+s^2*t^2. An allowed refinement
adds strict relations, retains every original cone relation, and keeps exactly
0 and 1 as minima. It does NOT change the geometric cone order's definition.

2. Build abstract V-then-chain orders, enumerate their antichains, and identify

3. Independently enumerate all directed pair-state assignments with the marked

The shortcut “real-cone comparison has the desired nonnesting enumerator” is
retired by the explicit I2(5) certificate. It is not attributed as a true claim

## Exact delta and proof steps

The cone has the eight strict relations

    0<2, 0<3, 0<4, 1<2, 1<3, 1<4, 2<4, 3<4.

Since phi>1, the difference of roots 2 and 3 has opposite-sign coordinates.
Thus {2,3} is an additional nonsimple antichain, alongside {0,1}. All other
antichains are empty or singletons. Consequently

    H_cone(s,t)=1+2st+3t+s^2*t^2+t^2,

with eight antichains rather than seven. Its orbits are

    empty -> {0,1} -> {2,3} -> {4} -> empty;
    {0} -> {1} -> {0};
    {2} -> {3} -> {2}.

To get the target polynomial without removing cone relations, the incomparable
pair {2,3} must become comparable; {0,1} must stay incomparable. Adding exactly
2<3 or exactly 3<2 suffices, is transitively closed with the old relations, and
is minimal. Adding both would create a cycle. Each resulting abstract order has
H=1+2st+3t+s^2*t^2 and orbit lengths [2,5]. These are the only two labelled
refinements meeting the target, and they are isomorphic by swapping 2 and 3.
They do not make either of those coordinate comparisons geometrically true.

For any m>=3, a poset with exactly the two marked minima and the target H has
exactly one incomparable pair, namely the two minima. Every other pair is
comparable; the m-2 other elements form a chain above both minima. Conversely,
that V-then-chain has precisely the target antichains. There are (m-2)! labelled
orders and one such abstract order up to a marking-preserving isomorphism.
This argument does not claim a canonical labelling of actual roots.

The abstract Panyushev map interchanges the two singleton minima. Its other
orbit is empty, the pair of minima, then successive chain singletons from
bottom to top, back to empty. Its length is m. This is an ordinary derived
all-m argument; finite tests do not substitute for its proof or kernel checking.
