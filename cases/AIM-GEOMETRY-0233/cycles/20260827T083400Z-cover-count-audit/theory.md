# A sharper conditional cover-count denominator

Agent output is unverified research assistance, not a mathematical result.

## Source and bounded target

The exact canonical statement in `snapshot.json` splices the final sentence of
Section 5.5 to the heading of Section 5.6 in Burns–Matveev, printed page 12.
The primary PDF confirms this boundary. The actual Question 5.6.1 follows the
heading and is not inserted into the canonical statement. Human scope review
is required; no imported status is changed.

The bounded target is the counting constant in the imported conditional
simple-lift concentration lemma. That lemma uses C_r(D)=sum_(d=1)^D (d!)^r.
We derive a smaller valid denominator from based-cover counts and provide exact
counting evidence. This is an elementary refinement/calibration, not a novelty
claim or a solution to a geodesic existence question.

## Definitions, quantifiers and assumptions

Let M be a connected smooth Riemannian manifold with fundamental group G
generated by r>=0 elements. Let D>=1 and N>=1 be integers. Assume N primitive
closed geodesics have distinct geometric images and each has a closed embedded
lift in some connected cover of M with degree at most D. All cover metrics are
pulled back from M. Cover equivalence means isomorphism over M, not merely a
homeomorphism of total spaces. Such isomorphisms preserve the pulled-back metric,
embeddedness and projections of lifts.

These are hypotheses, not outputs of the experiment. We construct no geodesic
family and make no assertion that a uniform D exists. Smooth connected manifolds
satisfy the local hypotheses of the covering classification theorem. If an
initial witness cover were disconnected, its component containing the lift would
still have degree at most D and may be used instead.

Write t_r(d) for the number of ordered r-tuples of permutations of a labelled
d-element set that act transitively. Write a_r(d) for the number of index-d
subgroups of the free group F_r, equivalently based connected degree-d covers of
the r-petal rose. Define B_r(D)=sum_(d=1)^D a_r(d). Based counts are an upper
bound for unbased cover classes, not their exact number.

## Two approaches

1. Count every assignment of r generators to S_d. This gives at most (d!)^r
   actions, including disconnected ones and repeated fibre labellings. It is
   sufficient for the imported coarse pigeonhole constant.
2. Remove disconnected actions by the orbit-of-a-root recurrence and remove
   repeated labellings by the free action of permutations fixing the root. This
   yields the exact based free-group count and a stronger universal upper bound.

We do not divide by d! to count unbased covers: the full relabelling action has
stabilizers. The verifier exhibits a degree-two failure of that shortcut.

## Counting derivation

Partition all (d!)^r tuples by the orbit of the distinguished label 0. If its
orbit has size k, choose its other k-1 labels, choose a transitive tuple on that
orbit, then choose any tuple on the complement. Therefore, for every d>=1,

    t_r(d) = (d!)^r
             - sum_(k=1)^(d-1) binom(d-1,k-1)*t_r(k)*((d-k)!)^r.

In particular t_r(1)=1, including r=0. The remaining (d-1)! relabellings fixing
0 act freely on transitive tuples: a permutation commuting with every generator
and fixing 0 fixes its whole orbit, hence every label. Thus

    a_r(d) = t_r(d)/(d-1)!.

This quotient counts subgroups, not just conjugacy classes: the stabilizer of 0
is unchanged under root-fixing relabelling, and the labelled coset action of any
index-d subgroup yields exactly such a class. For r=0, only a_0(1)=1 is nonzero;
for r=1, a_1(d)=1 for every d. No negative factorial exponents are required.

A surjection F_r -> G pulls each index-d subgroup of G back to a distinct
index-d subgroup of F_r. Hence the number of based degree-d covers of M is at
most a_r(d); the number of unbased classes is no larger. The topological
classification and degree/index correspondence are the explicit dependencies
in Hatcher, Proposition 1.32 and Theorem 1.38 (`sources.json`).

## Conditional concentration and quantitative change

Choose one representative of each unbased cover class of degree at most D.
Every assumed simple lift transfers to its representative. There are at most
B_r(D) representatives. Assign each of the N base geodesics to one representative
in which it has a simple lift. One representative receives at least

    ceil(N/B_r(D))

base geodesics. Their selected lifts have distinct geometric images: identical
lift images would project to identical base images. For an infinite family, the
same finite union argument gives infinitely many distinct simple lifts in one
fixed cover. Neither this argument nor primitiveness implies that simplicity
descends to the base. Primitiveness is retained from the imported statement,
although geometric distinctness is the part used in the counting step.

For r=2 and D=6, the exact based counts are

    1, 3, 13, 71, 461, 3447.

Thus B_2(6)=3996, whereas C_2(6)=533417. If a family satisfying all hypotheses
had N=533417 members, the improved guaranteed concentration is 134 rather than
1. This is a conditional arithmetic example, not an observed family of geodesics.

`theory_count.py` computes the recurrence for 50 rank/degree pairs (r=0..4,
d=1..10). Finite computations support and audit the arithmetic; the orbit and
coset arguments supply the general statement. The bound is not asserted sharp
for unbased covers or for any particular manifold.

## Falsification and limits

Check disconnected actions, invalid permutations, root preservation, stabilizers,
group relations, rank zero, degree one, and the ceiling boundary. A smaller
number of generators alone does not determine the actual cover count: relations
can reduce it. The verifier compares F_2 and Z^2 at degree 3 (13 versus 4 based
subgroups), confirming that the free count is an upper bound, not a universal
equality. Its unbased class counts are diagnostic and are not substituted into
the general B_r(D) formula here.

The exact missing geometric step is a uniformly bounded simple-lift degree for
the desired infinite family. Individual finite covers with unbounded degrees
do not permit the finite pigeonhole argument. Even an infinite simple family
upstairs does not by itself answer the base-manifold question. The source
fragment, geometry, independent human/model review and kernel formalization
remain unresolved obligations. No parent-problem or imported status is promoted.
